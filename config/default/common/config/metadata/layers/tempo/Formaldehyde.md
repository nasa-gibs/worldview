### Formaldehyde (HCHO)
Formaldehyde (HCHO) is a volatile organic compound (VOC) that is an important atmospheric pollutant and air quality indicator. Formaldehyde is emitted from both anthropogenic sources, such as vehicle emissions, industrial processes, power generation, and biomass burning and natural sources including vegetation and ocean emissions. Elevated formaldehyde concentrations indicate the presence of volatile organic compounds and their oxidation products in the atmosphere, signaling poor air quality conditions. Exposure to formaldehyde can cause respiratory irritation, eye discomfort, and other adverse health effects, with particular risks for sensitive populations. Formaldehyde also plays a crucial role in atmospheric chemistry as an intermediate product in the oxidation of other volatile organic compounds, influencing ozone formation and secondary pollutant generation. Since formaldehyde concentrations fluctuate based on emission sources, weather patterns, and photochemical processes, satellite-based near-real-time monitoring provides essential data for tracking air quality events, identifying pollution hotspots, and supporting environmental decision-making. Formaldehyde observations help scientists and air quality managers better understand the complex chemistry driving air pollution and develop strategies to protect public health.
